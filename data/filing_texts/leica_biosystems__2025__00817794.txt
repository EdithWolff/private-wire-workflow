PRINCIPAL ACTIVITIES

The company is a private, limited by shares organisation, that carries out the business of the sale and
service of surveying, measuring, positioning and guidance systems. Almost all revenue is in respect
of Leica branded products.

RESULTS AND DIVIDENDS

The company's profit for the financial year was £4,465,000 (2023 - £4,125,000). The company paid
an interim dividend of £3 per share amounting to £3,825,000 (2023 —- £8,925,000) in November 2024.
The directors do not propose the payment of a final dividend in respect of the year under review
(2023 — nil).

REVIEW OF BUSINESS, FUTURE DEVELOPMENTS AND KEY PERFORMANCE INDICATORS

Overall, the conditions of the construction market, which declined in the latter part of 2023, continued
into the first half of 2024. The market was challenging and volatile throughout the year. However,
conditions did begin to improve in its second half.

Despite these challenges, the company saw an increased level of adoption of its technology. As a
result of the foregoing the company saw a slight increase in turnover of 3% over the year as a whole.
Gross margin declined from 17% to 15% over the previous year. This was primarily driven by a shift
in the company's product mixture reflecting the dynamic market conditions.

While selling and administrative expenses increased by £1,249,000 during 2024, this were more than
offset by an increase of £1,690,000 in other operating income - thus reflecting an improvement in
operational efficiency.

In respect of corporation tax, the company benefitted from group relief surrendered by other group
companies. This resulted in a substantial credit to the profit and loss account for the year under
review.

The company further improved its management of credit processes and the collection of outstanding
balances from customers - despite the market conditions. As a result, trade debtors at the year end
were equivalent to 36 days’ sales — a reduction of 9 days compared to 2023.

The company is in a strong financial position and believes that government initiatives including large
infrastructure projects will support the construction industry and provide future opportunities for
growth. The development and release of new products and services by Leica Geosystems globally
will provide sustainable revenue streams in the future.

The company expects to report revenue growth during 2025. It is vigilant and agile in navigating the
challenging and quickly-evolving environment that impacts its markets.

PRINCIPAL FINANCIAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES

Credit risk is managed by agreeing payment terms in advance, including progress and upfront
payments. Appropriate credit control procedures are followed in line with group policy to minimise the
credit risk. Where credit risk is perceived to be high, customer contracts provide for payments

to be made prior to despatch of goods. The company has taken additional steps to ensure the prompt
collection of debt from its customers and distribution partners.

21. Significant Accounting Policies

Tangible fixed assets
The cost of fixed assets is the purchase cost, plus directly attributable costs. Fixed assets are stated

at their historic purchase price less accumulated depreciation and accumulated impairment loss,
Depreciation is provided on all tangible fixed assets at rates calculated to write off the cost less
estimated residual value of each asset evenly over its expected useful life, as follows:

Computer equipment 20% - 33% per annum
Leasehold improvements 10% per annum
Furniture, fixtures and equipment 15% - 20% per annum

The assets’ residual values, useful lives and depreciation methods are annually reviewed, and
adjusted prospectively if appropriate, or if there is an indication of significant change since the last
reporting date. The carrying values of tangible fixed assets are reviewed for impairment when events
or changes in circumstances indicate the carrying value may not be recoverable.

Stocks
Stocks are stated at the lower of cost, including freight and duty where applicable, and net realisable
value. Stocks are measured on weighted average cost basis.

Income taxes
Current tax assets and liabilities are measured at the amount expected to be recovered or paid to the
taxation authorities, based on tax rates and laws that are enacted or substantively enacted by the

balance sheet date.

Deferred income tax is recognised on all temporary differences arising between the tax bases of
assets and liabilities and their carrying amounts in the financial statements.

Deferred income tax assets are recognised only to the extent that it is probable that taxable profit will
be available against which the deductible temporary differences, carried forward tax credits or tax
losses can be utilised.

Deferred income tax assets and liabilities are measured on an undiscounted basis at the tax rates
that are expected to apply when the related asset or liability is settled, based on tax rates and laws
enacted or substantively enacted at the balance sheet date.

Foreign currency translation
The company's financial statements are presented in Sterling, which is also the company's functional

currency.

Foreign currency transactions during the year are translated into Sterling at the exchange rates ruling
at the date of the transactions. Monetary assets and liabilities at the year-end are translated at the
rates ruling at the balance sheet date and all differences are taken to the profit and loss account.

Share programmes

The company operates equity-settled share-based payment programmes in conjunction with its
ultimate parent company under which key employees are granted, free of charge, performance
awards which, subject to those employees’ continued employment with the group and certain
performance conditions, will ordinarily entitle them to shares of the ultimate parent company. The fair
value of those awards at the time of grant is expensed over their vesting periods of between three
and four years and is credited to equity.

Charges from the ultimate parent company for those awards are deducted from equity.

Trade and other debtors

Trade debtors are recognised and carried at the lower of their original invoiced value and recoverable
amount. Provision for impairment is made through profit or loss when there is objective evidence that
the company will not be able to recover balances in full. Balances are written off when the probability
of recovery is assessed as being remote.

Financial instruments

A financial instrument is any contract that gives rise to a financial asset of one entity and a financial
liability or equity instrument of another entity.

i) Financial assets

Financial assets are recognised when the entity becomes a party to the contract and, as a
consequence, has a legal right to receive cash.

All financial assets are initially measured at fair value plus, in the case of financial assets not
recorded at fair value through profit or loss, transaction costs that are attributable to the acquisition of
the financial asset. Purchases or sales of financial assets that require delivery of assets within a time
frame established by regulation or convention in the marketplace (regular way trades) are recognised
on the trade date being the date that the company commits to purchase or sell the asset.

All recognised financial assets are subsequently measured in their entirety at either amortised cost or
fair value, depending on the classification of the financial assets.

The company classifies its financial assets as at amortised cost only if the asset is held within a
business model whose objective is to collect the contractual cash flows and the contractual terms
give rise to cash flows that are solely payments of principal and interest. After initial measurement,
such financial assets are subsequently measured at amortised cost using the effective interest rate
(EIR) method. Amortised cost is calculated by taking into account any discount or premium on
acquisition and fees or costs that are an integral part of the EIR. The EIR amortisation is included in
finance income in the profit or loss. The losses arising from impairment are recognised in the profit or
loss.

The company applies the IFRS 9 simplified approach to measuring expected credit losses of trade
debtors by using a lifetime expected loss allowance.

ii) Financial liabilities

All financial liabilities are recognised initially at fair value and, in the case of loans and borrowings
and payables, net of directly attributable transaction costs.

After initial recognition, interest-bearing loans and borrowings are subsequently measured at
amortised cost using the EIR method. Gains and losses are recognised in profit or loss when the
liabilities are derecognised as well as through the EIR amortisation process. Amortised cost is
calculated by considering any discount or premium on acquisition and fees or costs that are an
integral part of the EIR. The EIR amortisation is included as finance costs in the statement of profit

and loss.

Leased and hired assets

Right-of-use assets and lease liabilities are recognised for all leases except those of low value assets
and those with a duration of 12 months or less. Right-of-use assets are measured at cost being the
initial measurement of the lease liability together with any payments made in advance of the lease
commencement date. Right-of-use assets are capitalised as a separate item on the balance sheet.

The company depreciates right-of-use assets on a straight-line basis from the lease commencement
date until the earlier of the end of life of the asset or the end of the lease term, whichever is the earlier.
Where impairment indicators exist the right-of-use asset will be assessed for impairment.

Lease liabilities are measured at the present value of the future lease payments discounted using the
interest rate implicit in the leases. If that rate could not be determined the group's incremental borrowing

rate is used.

Changes in lease contracts may give rise to a remeasurement of the liability and an adjustment to the
right-of-use asset.

Pensions

The company operates a defined contribution pension scheme for all qualifying employees. The
assets of the scheme are held separately from those of the company in an independent administered
fund. Contributions to defined contribution funds are charged to the profit and loss account in the
period to which they relate.

Provisions for liabilities

A provision is recognised when the company has a legal or constructive obligation as a result of a

past event, it is probable that an outflow of economic benefit will be required to settle the obligation
and a reliable estimate can be made of the amount of the obligation.

Revenue from contracts with customers

The company enters into contract arrangements with customers that contain multiple performance
obligations, such as transfer of goods, software and/or services. For these arrangements, management
judgment is applied to allocate revenue to each performance obligation, as these obligations are
fulfilled at different points in time or over time.

Sales of goods

Revenue is recognised when control of the goods is transferred to the customer, which arises at the
later of the goods being delivered to the customer and there being no unfilled obligation that could
affect the customer's acceptance of the goods. The amount of consideration equals the consideration

stated in the contract less any applicable rebates.

Sales of services, licences, rentals and other assignments

Revenue from the sale of services such as installations, maintenance and training is recognised in the
period in which the services are performed. Revenue from the sales of right-to-use licences is
recognised when the licence is transferred to the customer. Revenue from the sale of right-of-access
licences is recognised over the licence period. Revenue from the sale of software subscriptions is
recognised evenly over the subscription period. Revenue from the short-term hire of goods is
recognised evenly over the period to which the rental relates.

Financing components

The company does not expect to have any material contracts where the period between the transfer of
the promised goods or services to the customer and the payment by the customer exceeds one year.
Therefore, the company does not adjust any of the transaction prices for the time value of money.
