PRINCIPAL ACTIVITIES

Almirall Limited is a pharmaceutical company committed to health that bases its strategy on innovation and growth.
Its principal activities benefit healthcare professionals and patients within the United Kingdom via the marketing and
selling of pharmaceutical products from Almirall S.A. (“parent company”) to the UK market. As the parent company,
Almirall S.A., is responsible for setting the strategic direction and review of Almirall Ltd (“the subsidiary”)
performance to ensure it meets its targets and objectives.

REVIEW OF THE BUSINESS

The company has continued to invest in the growth of its promoted portfolio comprising of established dermatology
brands with the vision to become a leading dermatology player with a strong focus on improving the lives of
dermatology patients.

The total turnover in 2024 was £24,292,178 compared to £21,165,704 in 2023 reflecting good health of the mature
portfolio (mainly Solaraze®), the sales acceleration of Ilumetri® and Ebglyss® launch. Generic competition for
Solaraze®, the first product in sales of the company, entered the UK market in October 2019 but a successful price
strategy has been deployed to continue to maintain market share, forcing the generic off the market in 2024. The level
of generic competition in the rest of the mature portfolio remains extremely high and the company is challenged in
remaining competitive due to the increasing levels of commercial discounts required to keep the existing commercial
agreements.

The Company continues to have a strong balance sheet with net current assets as at 31 December 2024 of £12,404,324
(2023: £11,673,415) and total assets of £16,800,313 compared to £15,756,703 in 2023.

According to group transfer pricing policy, the operating profit on the distribution of products must necessarily be
around 2,6% of revenue, as agreed in the Advanced Pricing Agreement (APA) reached with the Spanish Tax
Authorities. Operating profit increased to £657,647 in 2024 (2023: £611,256) while total comprehensive income
amounted to £732,649 for the financial year 2024 (2023: £557,345).

The parent company delivered strong sales performance in 2024, as well as the continued investment in the
dermatology pipeline, and the evolution of our company culture, which set us up for sustained success. The parent
company have also demonstrated the commitment to addressing significant unmet needs in dermatology and to
improving patient outcomes. The parent company successfully delivered key launches in 2024, and an important
milestone in 2023 was obtaining the European approval of EBGLYSS® (lebrikizumab), our new biological treatment
for moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis. EBGLYSS® is available in the United Kingdom since July 2024. The
investment in Research & Development allows us to create new promising assets through in-house research, game-
changing collaborations, and licensing deals.

KEY PERFORMANCE INDICATORS

Revenue is the main Key Performance Indicator as it drives the calculation of the transfer pricing adjustment that
determines the operating profit of the Company, according to the Advanced Pricing Agreement (APA) reached with
the Spanish Tax Authorities.

There are no significant non-financial Key Performance Indicators.

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES

The director has considered the current economic climate in relation to the pharmaceutical industry, and he believes
that the impact of a potential future recession on the company is likely to be minimal. In common with the rest of the
pharmaceutical sector the company's key risks relate to the expiry of product patents, the threat of new and existing
competitor products and the risk of government price cuts. The parent company’s investment in new products and its
sales and marketing activity will allow Almirall Limited to maintain and gain market share for both new and existing
brands.

Products are well positioned within their therapeutic areas and there is reasonable certainty around NHS pricing levels
driven by the 2019 Pharmaceutical price regulation scheme (VPAS) agreement. A new scheme was signed in 2024
named as the Voluntary Scheme for Pricing Access and Growth (VPAG) which maintains the financial situation and
gives stability until 2028. The principal objective of the new scheme is to promote better patient outcomes, support
United Kingdom economic growth and financial sustainability of the NHS.

Given the group's investment in Almirall Limited and the profitable nature of the UK business, the director does not
believe there to be a significant risk of amounts due to group undertakings being called in a manner which would
create liquidity issues for the company. The continuing conflict between Russia and Ukraine since early 2022 has had
no significant adverse impact on the financial performance and financial position of the company.

Health System Contribution accrual (VPAG)

The 2024 Voluntary Scheme for Branded Medicines Pricing, Access and Growth ‘VPAG’ is a commitment by
industry, the NHS and Government to support innovation for the benefit of patients across the UK. This scheme strikes
a balance between supporting innovation in the pharmaceutical industry, helping to get the most cost-effective
medicines to patients as quickly as possible, and ensuring complete predictability on spend for the entire branded
medicines bill for the NHS. The VPAG means that the branded medicines bill will start at 2% in 2024 and rise to 4%
in 2027-2028. As agreed with the Government, the Voluntary Scheme states that the pharmaceutical industry will
reimburse to the Department of Health and Social Care (DHSC) any incremental growth via rebates.
c

The company records an accrual based on a percentage of eligible sales. The VPAG accrual involves the use of a
significant assumption, being the accuracy of the estimated growth rate embedded in the annual VPAG payment rate
communicated by DHSC. As such, the VPAG accrual consists of an estimate of the amount expected to be paid for
the current year eligible sales at the official payment rate communicated by DHSC, plus an accrual for estimated
underpaid or overpaid rebates from prior years, resulting from the comparison of real growth versus estimated growth
included in the official rate. This under/overpayment is payable/recoverable to DHSC at the end of scheme
reconciliation.

Other operating income

According to group transfer pricing policy, the operating profit on the distribution of products must necessarily be
around 2.6% of revenue, as agreed in the Advanced Pricing Agreement (APA) reached with the Spanish and UK Tax
Authorities. This is guaranteed via the intercompany reinvocing to the parent company Almirall, S.A. of marketing
services classified as other operating income in the Statement of comprehensive income. These marketing services
include all expenses related to the commercialization of Almirall products in the UK (direct selling expenses, sales

and marketing administration, advertising and promotion). Outside the scope of the APA, additional activities are also
performed and reinvoiced to the parent company with a mark-up over costs (R&D services and others) and included
in this caption.

Property, plant and equipment

Property, plant and equipment are stated at cost or valuation, net of depreciation and any provision for impairment.
Cost includes the original purchase price of the asset and the costs attributable to bringing the asset to its working
condition for its intended use. Depreciation is provided on cost using the reducing balance method over the estimated
useful lives of the assets. The rates of depreciation are as follows:

Leasehold improvements - 20.0%
Computer equipment - 33.3%

Current and deferred income tax

The tax expense for the year comprises current and deferred tax. Tax is recognised in the income statement, except to
the extent that it relates to items recognised in other comprehensive income or directly in shareholders’ funds. In this
case, the tax is also recognised in other comprehensive income or directly in shareholders’ funds, respectively.

The current income tax charge is calculated on the basis of the tax laws enacted or substantively enacted at the balance
sheet date in the countries where the company operates and generates taxable income. Management periodically
evaluates positions taken in tax returns with respect to situations in which applicable tax regulation is subject to
interpretation. It establishes provisions where appropriate on the basis of amounts expected to be paid to the tax
authorities.

Deferred income tax is recognised on temporary differences arising between the tax bases of assets and liabilities and
their carrying amounts in the financial statements. However, deferred tax liabilities are not recognised if they arise
from the initial recognition of goodwill; or arise from initial recognition of an asset or liability in a transaction other
than a business combination that at the time of the transaction affects neither accounting nor taxable profit or loss.
Deferred income tax is determined using tax rates (and laws) that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the
balance sheet date and are expected to apply when the related deferred income tax asset is realised or the deferred
income tax liability is settled. ~

Inventories

Inventories are stated at the lower of cost and net realisable value. Cost is determined using the weighted average cost
method. Under this method, the cost of each item of inventory is based on the average cost of similar items at the
beginning of a period and the cost of similar items purchased or produced during the period. The cost of goods
available for sale is divided by the number of units available for sale to determine the weighted average cost per unit.
This cost is then used to determine the cost of ending inventory and the cost of goods sold.

Provision is made for obsolete, slow-moving or defective items where appropriate.

At the end of each reporting period inventories are assessed for impairment. If an item of inventory is impaired, the
identified inventory is reduced to its selling price less costs to complete and sell, and an impairment charge is
recognised in the profit and loss account.

Cash and cash equivalents includes cash in hand, deposits held at call with banks and other short-term highly liquid
investments with original maturities of three months or less. In the balance sheet, bank overdrafts are shown within
borrowings in current liabilities.

Leases

The company's lease contracts consist mainly of property lease for the office premises and transport elements. The
office lease contract is for a period of 10 years with a Break-Up Clause at the end of year 5.

Leases are recognised as a right-of-use asset and a corresponding liability at the date at which the leased asset is
available for use by the company. Each lease payment is allocated between the liability and the finance cost. The
finance cost is charged to the statement of comprehensive income over the lease period so as to produce a constant
periodic rate of interest on the remaining balance of the liability for each period. The right-of-use asset is depreciated
over the shorter of the asset's useful life and the lease term on a straight-line basis.

Assets and liabilities arising from a lease are initially measured on a present value basis. Lease liabilities include the
net present value of the following lease payments:

fixed payments (including in-substance fixed payments), less any lease incentives receivable

variable lease payments that are based on an index or a rate

amounts expected to be payable by the lessee under residual value guarantees

the exercise price of a purchase option if the lessee is reasonably certain to exercise that option, and
payments of penalties for terminating the lease, if the lease term reflects the lessee exercising that option.

The lease payments are discounted using the interest rate implicit in the lease. If that rate cannot be determined, the
lessee's incremental borrowing rate is used, being the rate that the lessee would have to pay to borrow the funds
necessary to obtain an asset of similar value in a similar economic environment with similar terms, securities and
conditions.

Right-of-use assets are measured at cost comprising the following:

the amount of the initial measurement of lease liability

any lease payments made at or before the commencement date less any lease incentives received
any initial direct costs, and

restoration costs

Payments associated with short-term leases and leases of low-value assets are recognised on a straight line basis as

or less. Low-value assets comprise IT-equipment.
